How it works

Reading a hand, one point at a time

A camera watches for a hand. A pretrained model locates 21 key points on it — the same landmarks a physiotherapist would mark. A classifier, trained on hand signs recorded specifically for this system, decides what those points mean. If it's an emergency word, it says so immediately.

About us

Communication should never be the barrier.

Bridge Lite is a final year AI project that reads hand signs through a camera and turns them into text and spoken voice, and turns typed words back into the sign. It is built for the moments when speech is not an option, especially in an emergency.

Who it is for

Built around the people who use it.

Primary users

People with Hearing Disabilities

People who use sign language as their main way of communicating, and need their signs understood quickly by people who do not understand sign language.

Primary users

People with Speech Disabilities

People who cannot speak clearly or at all and who communicate through gestures. The system gives them a spoken voice in real time.

Supporting Users

Families and Caregivers

Relatives and caregivers who want a simple way to talk with a loved one who signs, without waiting for an interpreter.

Supporting Users

Hospital and Public Service Staff

Front-desk and emergency staff who need to understand urgent requests from someone who signs, without a delay.

Mission and Vision

Why Bridge Lite exists.

Mission

Understood the moment it matters

To give Deaf, hard-of-hearing and speech-impaired people a way to be understood immediately, without waiting for an interpreter, starting with the places where delay matters most.

Vision

Communication is never the barrier

A world where sign language is understood by the technology people already carry, in hospitals, classrooms, homes and public offices alike.

Limits and next steps

Future progress

Where the technology can go next.

01

Wearables

Meta glasses and smart watches

Runs as a background application on Meta glasses and smart watches, interpreting conversations around the user in real time. This is one-way: it translates what others sign or say to the user, but cannot translate the user's signs or text back to the other person. Useful for educational settings.

02

Smart homes

Sign-controlled IoT

Most smart homes rely on voice assistants. A sign interpreter can convert the user's sign commands into speech or text commands for smart appliances, giving users an assistant they can control entirely with sign language.

03

Browser extensions

Live translation for media

Delivered as a browser extension, providing real-time translation for videos and audio the user is watching or listening to.

04

Desktop, mobile and tablet

Background interpretation apps

Applications that run in the background on desktop, mobile and tablet devices and provide real-time interpretation throughout the day.
